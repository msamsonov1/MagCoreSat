Electromagnetic & Thermodynamic Govering Formulations

Rigorous mathematical models governing high-temperature permeability drop, modified Steinmetz thermal dissipation, and multiphase load-sharing inversion for lunar planar transformers.

1. Temperature-Dependent Flux Saturation

In amorphous alloy ($Fe_{78}Si_9B_{13}$) and high-frequency ferrite planar cores, spontaneous magnetic domain alignment collapses as the core approaches its Curie temperature $T_c$. The saturation flux density follows the modified mean-field Bloch-Weiss relation:

$$B_{sat}(T) = B_{0}\left(1 - \frac{T}{T_c}\right)^\beta \cdot \left[1 - \alpha_T(T - T_{ref})\right]$$

Where $B_0 \approx 1.56\,\text{T}$ at cryogenic lunar night ($-180^\circ\text{C}$), critical exponent $\beta \approx 0.36$, Curie temperature $T_c \approx 395^\circ\text{C}$, and thermal degradation coefficient $\alpha_T \approx 0.0028\,\text{K}^{-1}$. At peak lunar noon ($120^\circ\text{C}$ ambient, $145^\circ\text{C}$ junction), $B_{sat}$ plummets by more than 45%.

2. Thermal Steinmetz Core Dissipation

Volumetric core loss $P_v$ escalates non-linearly with both operating peak flux $B_{pk}$ and switching frequency $f_{sw}$. In lunar vacuum environments lacking convective cooling, radiative heat rejection governs junction equilibrium:

$$P_v(T, B) = k \cdot f_{sw}^\alpha \cdot B_{pk}^\gamma \cdot \left(c_0 - c_1 T + c_2 T^2\right) + \sigma_{eff} \frac{\pi^2 d^2}{6 \rho_c} f_{sw}^2 B_{pk}^2$$

The classic minimum in ferrite loss occurs near $80^\circ\text{C}$. Beyond $100^\circ\text{C}$, the quadratic coefficient $c_2 T^2$ dominates, creating positive feedback: elevated temperature spikes hysteresis losses, raising temperature further toward catastrophic magnetic runaway.

3. Dynamic Interleave Phase-Shift Inversion

When core observer telemetry detects a diminishing margin $M(t) = B_{sat}(T) - B_{pk}(t) \le \epsilon_{critical}$, the digital signal processor alters the PWM gate drive timing matrix $\mathbf{\Phi}$ across $N$ interleaved converter legs:

$$B_{pk,k} = \frac{V_{in} \cdot D_k}{2 N_{turn} A_c f_{sw}} \cdot \left[ 1 - \sum_{j \ne k} \kappa_{jk} \sin\left(\phi_k - \phi_j\right) \right]$$

Where $\kappa_{jk}$ is the inter-core mutual flux coupling coefficient and $\phi$ represents the dynamic firing angle. By modulating $\phi$ from symmetric star ($120^\circ$) to asymmetric multi-channel pulse-skipping, MagCoreSat drops peak individual leg flux by up to 42% without lowering aggregate power delivery.

4. Lunar Surface Vacuum Heat Rejection

Lunar regolith has ultra-low thermal conductivity ($k_{reg} \approx 0.002\,\text{W/m}\cdot\text{K}$). Heat from the planar core must pass entirely through baseplate heat-pipes to a zenith-facing vertical space radiator:

$$C_{th} \frac{dT_{core}}{dt} = P_{core}(B, T) + P_{cu}(I) - \varepsilon \sigma_{SB} A_{rad} \left(T_{rad}^4 - T_{space}^4\right) - \frac{T_{core} - T_{coldplate}}{R_{cond}}$$

Where $\sigma_{SB} = 5.67 \times 10^{-8}\,\text{W/m}^2\text{K}^4$, emissivity $\varepsilon \ge 0.91$, and deep space sink $T_{space} \approx 4\,\text{K}$. At lunar midday, the solar irradiation incident on the substation radiator ($1361\,\text{W/m}^2$) throttles net rejection delta.
